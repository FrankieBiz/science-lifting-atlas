## Practical takeaway

The pectoralis major is one of the shoulder's strongest muscles for pulling the arm
down and across the body `[claim-pectoralis-major-adduction]`. It is not one uniform
sheet: its regions do measurably different amounts of work, and which region works
hardest depends on where your arm is
`[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-differentiation]`,
`[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-activation-varies-with-position]`. Chest resistance
training makes it measurably thicker, though the evidence for that is thinner than
you would expect
`[claim-pectoralis-major-hypertrophy-with-chest-resistance-training]`.

## Structure

The muscle arises from the anterior surface of the medial half of the clavicle, the
anterior surface of the sternum, the upper costal cartilages and the aponeurosis of
the external oblique, and inserts on the lateral lip of the intertubercular sulcus of
the humerus `[claim-pectoralis-major-attachments]`.

It is conventionally divided into a clavicular portion and a sternocostal portion,
named for their origins, with an abdominal part described as a third division
`[claim-pectoralis-major-portions]`. That two-part division is a simplification: the
sternocostal head has been described as having between two and seven distinct
segments, and the abdominal part has its own traceable nerve supply
`[claim-pectoralis-major-portions]`.

At the humerus the tendon has a tall, narrow footprint — measured in fourteen
cadaveric specimens at 75 mm (SD 9) from top to bottom and 7 mm (SD 1) across — with
the clavicular head tendon shorter than the sternal head tendon
`[claim-pectoralis-major-humeral-footprint]`.

**Where the standard description is contested.** The insertion is usually described as
having two layers. One cadaveric series that imaged and then sectioned the same
fourteen specimens found a single-layer fibrocartilaginous attachment in every one,
and none of ultrasound, MRI or histology identified the layers
`[claim-pectoralis-major-enthesis-unilaminar]`. Two further primary sources record
that the layered description remains controversial and that there is little consensus
on this muscle's architecture `[claim-pectoralis-major-enthesis-unilaminar]`. This
page reports the disagreement rather than choosing a side.

**Variation is common.** The typical configuration was found in 63.75 per cent of 80
dissected specimens, with a separate clavicular portion the most frequent departure
`[claim-pectoralis-major-structural-variation]`.

## Innervation

The pectoral nerves supply the muscle, with the lateral pectoral nerve supplying the
proximal part and the medial pectoral nerve the lower part
`[claim-pectoralis-major-innervation]`.

That clean account is a simplification, and this page says so because the primary
dissection literature says so. A Sihler-stained series of 80 specimens reports the
muscle as mainly innervated by the lateral pectoral nerve, with an intercostal
contribution present in every stained specimen; two independent series report three
constant pectoral branches rather than two; and the medial nerve's course through
pectoralis minor is reported as present in 62 per cent of people and absent in 38 per
cent `[claim-pectoralis-major-innervation]`.

## Function

The pectoralis major is among the muscles with the largest measured adductor moment
arms at the glenohumeral joint, alongside the latissimus dorsi and teres major
`[claim-pectoralis-major-adduction]`. Moment arm is leverage, not force: a large
moment arm does not by itself establish how much a muscle contributes to a movement
`[claim-pectoralis-major-adduction]`.

### The muscle does not act as one unit

Five independent kinds of measurement agree that the regions of the pectoralis major
behave differently from one another: measured moment arms differ significantly
between its sub-regions; regional and high-density electromyography finds
heterogeneous activity across its breadth and independent segmental control; and
tissue-stiffness measurement finds the regions differ in passive stretch response and
in activation-dependent stiffness `[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-differentiation]`.

One high-density study of twenty healthy females found the middle sternocostal region
activating 12 to 108 per cent more than the clavicular and superior sternocostal
regions during extension, during adduction with external rotation and during
high-elevation internal rotation `[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-differentiation]`.

Which region dominates depends on where the humerus is
`[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-activation-varies-with-position]`. In the bench
press, a 45-degree incline reverses which head is more excited relative to a flat
bench `[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-activation-varies-with-position]`.

**What this does not establish.** Regional differences in activation are not evidence
that a region can be grown selectively by training
`[claim-pectoralis-major-regional-differentiation]`. The outcome evidence for
regional growth in this slice is a single site-specific result in one small trial.

## Training response

Eight weeks of supervised chest resistance training increased pectoralis major muscle
thickness in untrained and recreationally active adults, in two small trials using
different exercises `[claim-pectoralis-major-hypertrophy-with-chest-resistance-training]`.

The certainty on that is **low**, and the reasons are worth stating rather than
hiding: two usable trials, both small; eight further eligible trials could not be
read in full; measurement is by ultrasound at one or three sites, and the imaging
plane, the site and the operator's experience each change the number obtained; and
one of the two trials prints identical values for both of its randomised arms, so
only the direction of its result is used
`[claim-pectoralis-major-hypertrophy-with-chest-resistance-training]`.

## How this muscle is measured, and why that matters

Standard bipolar surface electromyography mischaracterises pectoralis major activity
in common tasks, and innervation-zone positions shift within the muscle during the
bench press, so single-site surface EMG amplitude comparisons for this muscle are not
reliably comparable `[claim-pectoralis-major-surface-emg-limitation]`.

This is not a footnote. It is why the activation literature for this muscle
contradicts itself, and it is why every activation statement on this page and on the
linked exercise pages carries a qualifier
`[claim-pectoralis-major-surface-emg-limitation]`.
