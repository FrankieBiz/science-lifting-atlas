## Practical takeaway

The bench press loads the pectoralis major, and it does so unevenly across the
muscle: bench angle, grip and range of motion change which part is working
`[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-activation]`,
`[claim-bench-press-inclination-shifts-regional-activation]`. Eight weeks of
bench-press training measurably thickened the pectoralis major in small trials, at
low certainty `[claim-pectoralis-major-hypertrophy-with-chest-resistance-training]`.
Whether it builds more chest muscle than a cable fly is **unknown**: no study has
compared them `[absence-primary-comparison-no-evidence]`.

## Definition used by this project

**This section is a scoping definition, not an extracted fact.** It is reproduced from
`research/questions/SBLA-008-vertical-slice.md` §3.1 so that a reader can tell which
studies count as this exercise.

Defining attributes: a single straight barbell loaded with free weight; a horizontal
bench, backrest 0° ± 5°; supine with torso and hips supported and feet in contact with
the floor or a platform; pronated grip, both hands outside shoulder width; concentric
shoulder horizontal adduction and/or flexion **with** elbow extension; the bar
descends to contact or near-contact with the torso and returns to full or near-full
elbow extension; resistance from gravity acting on the barbell.

Recorded modifiers: grip width, bar touch point, paused versus touch-and-go, cadence,
degree of lumbar extension, prescribed scapular position, load, repetitions, sets,
rest, frequency, proximity to failure, supervision, programme duration, bar path and
spotter use.

Not this exercise: Smith-machine bench press, chest-press machine, dumbbell bench
press, incline or decline press outside 0° ± 5°, floor press, deliberate partial-range
or board press, accommodating resistance, unilateral press, push-up and every
bodyweight press.

## What the muscles do

The pectoralis major is measurably activated during the barbell flat bench press, at
both its clavicular and sternocostal regions
`[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-activation]`.

Electromyography measures activation, not force and not growth. No activation finding
on this page is evidence of hypertrophy, and single-site surface EMG over this
particular muscle is known to mischaracterise its activity
`[claim-pectoralis-major-surface-emg-limitation]`.

## Mechanics

During the ascent the moment arm of the bar about the shoulder axis decreases
continuously, while the moment arm about the elbow axis reaches its minimum at the
sticking region `[claim-bench-press-shoulder-moment-arm-decreases]`. The external
demand of this lift is therefore not constant through a repetition.

Two constraints travel with any mechanical figure for this exercise: joint moments
computed from vertical barbell force alone are incomplete, because lateral barbell
forces matter; and net joint moments differ with the lifter's training expertise and
sex, so a published moment value is a property of a technique as much as of the
exercise `[claim-bench-press-shoulder-moment-arm-decreases]`.

## Setup variables that change what is loaded

**Bench angle.** Raising the bench inclination shifts activation within the
pectoralis major toward the clavicular region and away from the sternocostal region
`[claim-bench-press-inclination-shifts-regional-activation]`. One small trial found a
matching site-specific thickness difference after eight weeks, at the upper
measurement site `[claim-bench-press-inclination-shifts-regional-activation]`. The
researchers who have measured this most carefully state in print that the inclination
literature is contradictory and that measurement artefact is a likely reason, and
one study of elite competitors found no significant activation difference between
flat, inclined and declined positions at all
`[claim-bench-press-inclination-shifts-regional-activation]`.

**Grip width.** The evidence is mixed. One isometric study found that grip width and
forearm rotation shifted activation between the muscle's portions; a study of
national and international competitors found no significant difference in pectoralis
activation across three grip widths at 6RM, while the 6RM load itself changed by 5.8
to 11.1 per cent `[claim-bench-press-grip-width-activation-mixed]`. This page does
not recommend a grip width for chest development, because the evidence does not
support one.

## Training response

Eight weeks of supervised chest resistance training increased pectoralis major muscle
thickness in untrained and recreationally active adults, in two small trials; one of
those trials used the bench press
`[claim-pectoralis-major-hypertrophy-with-chest-resistance-training]`. Certainty is
low and the limits are listed with the claim.

**Strength transfer.** In one eight-week volume-equated trial, a programme built
entirely from multi-joint exercises produced a larger increase in one-repetition
maximum bench press than a programme built entirely from single-joint exercises
(10.9 versus 8.1 per cent) `[claim-multi-joint-versus-single-joint-programme-strength]`.
That result is about a bench press test in a group that trained the bench press, in
soccer players, with no chest-size measurement taken, and it is not evidence about
chest muscle growth `[claim-multi-joint-versus-single-joint-programme-strength]`.

## Safety context

Rupture of the pectoralis major tendon is reported to occur during the bench press,
and published case reports describe it happening during a bench press manoeuvre
`[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-rupture]`.

Case reports and pooled case series have no denominator, so nothing here supports a
rate, a risk, or a comparison between exercises
`[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-rupture]`. This is context. This project does not give
medical, diagnostic or rehabilitation advice.

## Comparison with the cable fly

**No study has compared this exercise with a bilateral standing cable fly at shoulder
height for pectoralis major size** `[absence-primary-comparison-no-evidence]`.

What does exist is a mechanical comparison against a cable cross-over, which is a
related condition rather than the index cable fly: at matched percentages of body
weight the cross-over produced significantly larger maximum shoulder joint moments
and a far smaller elbow excursion than this exercise
`[claim-cable-crossover-shoulder-moment-larger-than-bench-press]`,
`[claim-cable-crossover-elbow-excursion-smaller-than-bench-press]`. Activation
comparisons against a dumbbell fly disagree with each other
`[claim-press-versus-fly-activation-mixed]`.
