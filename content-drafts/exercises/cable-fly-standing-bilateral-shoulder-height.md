## Practical takeaway

**Nobody has studied this exercise.** A search across PubMed, Europe PMC, OpenAlex,
ClinicalTrials.gov and three preprint routes, using thirty phrase variants for the
fly family, retrieved no study in which a bilateral standing cable fly at shoulder
height was a trained or measured condition
`[absence-index-cable-fly-no-evidence]`.

That is not a verdict on the exercise. It is a statement about the literature. What
follows is what can honestly be said from related conditions, each labelled as
related rather than as evidence about this exercise.

## Definition used by this project

**This section is a scoping definition, not an extracted fact.** It is reproduced from
`research/questions/SBLA-008-vertical-slice.md` §3.2.

Defining attributes: two cable stacks or pulleys, one per hand, each with a single
handle; both pulleys at approximately shoulder height; standing, split or parallel
stance, torso upright to slightly forward-inclined; the elbow held at a fixed,
slightly flexed angle throughout with **no intentional elbow extension**; concentric
shoulder horizontal adduction with the hands travelling toward each other in front of
the torso; resistance from cable tension, with the line of force set by the cable
path rather than by the gravity vector alone.

Recorded modifiers: pulley height in absolute or normalised terms, cable path length,
stance, torso inclination, elbow angle in degrees and how it was controlled,
end-range and start-range hand position, load, repetitions, sets, rest, frequency,
proximity to failure, supervision, programme duration, machine make and model, and
whether the stack used a cam or a constant-radius pulley.

Not this exercise: pec deck or machine fly, dumbbell fly on a bench, cable fly
performed lying supine, high-to-low and low-to-high crossover variants, single-arm
cable fly, banded fly, and any fly performed with intentional elbow extension.

## What related conditions can support

Each statement below is about a **related condition**, not about the index exercise,
and is labelled accordingly.

### A cable cross-over of unreported pulley height

At matched external loads of 15 and 30 per cent of body weight, a standing cable
cross-over produced significantly larger maximum absolute shoulder joint moments than
a flat barbell bench press: 0.650 (SD 0.113) against 0.442 (SD 0.046) Nm/kg at 15 per
cent of body weight, and 1.026 (SD 0.192) against 0.760 (SD 0.079) Nm/kg at 30 per
cent `[claim-cable-crossover-shoulder-moment-larger-than-bench-press]`.

Elbow flexion–extension range of motion was far smaller in the cross-over than in the
press: 21.8 (SD 10.9) degrees against 79.5 (SD 6.2) degrees at 15 per cent of body
weight `[claim-cable-crossover-elbow-excursion-smaller-than-bench-press]`.

**Why this is a related condition and not this exercise.** The study does not report
the pulley height, did not prescribe the elbow angle, and does not describe the
stance. Three of the defining attributes above are therefore unreported, and the
project's screening rule resolves that ambiguity away from the index set
`[claim-cable-crossover-shoulder-moment-larger-than-bench-press]`.

**Further limits.** Loads were set as a percentage of body weight rather than of 1RM,
so the relative intensity of the two exercises is unknown; the shoulder was modelled
without a separate scapula; a joint moment is an external demand, not a muscle force
or a growth stimulus; and this is one study of twenty participants
`[claim-cable-crossover-shoulder-moment-larger-than-bench-press]`.

### A machine butterfly

Eight weeks of machine butterfly training, five sets of 10 to 12 repetitions at a
10-12RM load three times weekly, increased pectoralis major muscle thickness compared
with a non-training control in recreationally active adults
`[claim-machine-fly-increases-pectoralis-thickness]`.

In the same trial, fifteen minutes a day of pectoral static stretching produced a
statistically indistinguishable thickness change, which constrains how confidently
the result can be attributed to the resistance training specifically
`[claim-machine-fly-increases-pectoralis-thickness]`.

A machine butterfly moves on a fixed path against a machine's own resistance curve.
It is not a cable fly `[claim-machine-fly-increases-pectoralis-thickness]`.

### A dumbbell fly

The clavicular portion of the pectoralis major is activated during a dumbbell fly
`[claim-dumbbell-fly-clavicular-activation]`. The supporting studies used
two-channel instrumentation and report no numeric activation values in their
retrievable text, and electromyography is never evidence of growth
`[claim-dumbbell-fly-clavicular-activation]`.

Acute activation comparisons between a barbell bench press and a dumbbell fly
disagree with each other: one found higher pectoralis activation in the press across
most movement phases, another found no significant difference in activation level and
only a shorter relative time of activation in the fly
`[claim-press-versus-fly-activation-mixed]`.

## Safety context

A published case report describes a humeral rupture of the pectoralis major in a
33-year-old man of normal habitus who felt sudden pectoral pain and heard a whip-like
popping sound while training on a chest fly machine
`[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-rupture]`.

One case establishes that the injury can occur on a fly machine and nothing about how
often. The report is in German and was read through machine-assisted translation
without a second independent check, so it cannot carry a decision-critical statement
on its own `[claim-bench-press-pectoralis-rupture]`. This is context, not medical,
diagnostic or rehabilitation advice.

## Comparison with the bench press

There is no tier-1 comparison to report
`[absence-primary-comparison-no-evidence]`. The pre-specified sensitivity analysis
for within-participant contralateral-limb designs is also empty: no retrieved study
trains one limb with the press and the other with a fly
`[absence-contralateral-sensitivity-analysis-empty]`.

The only same-participants, same-instrument comparison anywhere in this evidence set
is mechanical, and it is against a cable cross-over of unreported setup
`[claim-cable-crossover-shoulder-moment-larger-than-bench-press]`.

two terms in either order, so `"machine fly"[tiab:~0]` retrieves "on-the-fly machine
learning" papers; and Europe PMC supports a proximity operator that the governing E1
string does not use, so E1 misses inverted word orders. Both are recorded in
`research/searches/SBLA-009-search-receipts.json` and routed to the owning role.
