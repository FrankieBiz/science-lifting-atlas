# SBLA-012 anatomy production hold

Status: **active owner hold**  
Recorded: **2026-09-17**  
Authority: **human owner**  
Applies to: **SBLA-013, SBLA-014, and SBLA-015**

## Decision

The Science-Based Lifting Atlas will pursue an extremely high-quality body and
anatomy presentation, but production Blender and interactive 3D work will wait
until further notice. The hold protects that quality target from being reduced
to fit the current schedule and prevents an evaluation asset from becoming the
finished body by inertia.

SBLA-012 continues. Its static home, muscle, exercise, source, methodology, and
evidence journeys must establish the information architecture, visual language,
responsive behavior, accessibility contract, and usability findings that the
future anatomy experience will inherit.

## Work paused

While this hold is active, agents must not:

- install or configure Blender for production;
- begin the SBLA-013 Blender/glTF/media pipeline;
- manually or programmatically modify production anatomy meshes;
- create production materials, textures, lighting rigs, cameras, LODs, or GLB
  bundles;
- implement the SBLA-014 Three.js/React Three Fiber anatomy engine;
- present the existing BodyParts3D evaluation render as final-quality anatomy;
- purchase or substitute a body asset to avoid waiting; or
- silently lower the Clinical Cinematic visual, accessibility, licensing, or
  performance requirements.

Read-only inspection of the already accepted SBLA-004 through SBLA-006 evidence
is allowed when it is needed to keep static UI boundaries honest. The current
licensed poster may remain a clearly labelled, non-interactive evaluation
fallback in SBLA-012.

## Work that continues

Codex may continue the ordered SBLA-012 work on:

- the Luminous Anatomy design system and reusable static components;
- realistic muscle, exercise, source, and methodology archetypes;
- approved claim, qualifier, evidence, and source presentation;
- semantic SVG/text and static-image fallback contracts;
- keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, reflow, and no-JavaScript behavior;
- desktop and mobile visual regression coverage;
- the 3–5 participant formative usability study and resulting critical fixes;
- documentation and handoff material needed for independent Claude review; and
- non-3D performance and portability measurement.

Completing SBLA-012 does not automatically release SBLA-013. If SBLA-012 reaches
acceptance while this hold remains active, the ordered queue pauses before
SBLA-013 rather than skipping ahead to SBLA-016 or content production.

## Quality preserved for restart

The eventual anatomy body must still satisfy the accepted direction:

- museum-grade Clinical Cinematic/Luminous Anatomy presentation;
- evidence-reviewed semantic 2D/text meaning for every required target;
- optional progressive 3D enhancement rather than a canvas-only experience;
- stable entity-to-mesh mapping and deterministic asset provenance;
- excellent materials, lighting, silhouette, labeling, and selection states;
- desktop, mobile, low-power, no-WebGL, reduced-motion, keyboard, and
  screen-reader paths;
- lawful sources, complete attribution, versioned manifests, and checksums; and
- measured payload, memory, frame-time, and visual-quality gates.

The hold is a scheduling decision, not permission to redesign these standards.

## Release conditions

Only a new explicit owner decision committed to the repository may release this
hold. That decision should state:

1. whether the accepted BodyParts3D hybrid remains the production direction or
   a new asset evaluation is authorized;
2. the quality references and body-presentation requirements to reproduce;
3. the approved Blender version and production environment;
4. the exact first vertical slice and success criteria;
5. any authorized purchase or licensing budget;
6. who will perform interactive visual inspection and scientific review; and
7. whether the release covers SBLA-013 only or also authorizes later 3D tasks.

Until all seven items are explicit, future agents must treat the hold as active.
