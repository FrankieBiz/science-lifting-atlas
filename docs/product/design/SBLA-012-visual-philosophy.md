# Applied Strength Editorial

Applied Strength Editorial is the visual direction for the Science-Based Lifting
Atlas. Its promise is simple: **research you can train with**. The interface
should feel like an exceptionally clear training manual backed by a serious
research desk—not a museum installation, science-fiction control room, medical
dashboard, or neon fitness SaaS product. Every visual decision must help a
lifter understand the practical takeaway, judge its certainty, and inspect the
evidence behind it.

## Information hierarchy

Pages lead with the user's training question and the shortest useful answer.
The default reading path is:

1. identify the muscle, exercise, or source;
2. understand what matters in practice;
3. see the certainty, applicability, and important limitation;
4. inspect the supporting, qualifying, or contradictory evidence; and
5. follow a clear route to related records and methodology.

Evidence detail is progressive, not hidden. Takeaways must remain concise and
useful, while claim text, exact locators, source status, limitations, and
revision information stay available in the same page flow. The layout must not
bury core guidance beneath a giant hero, ornamental negative space, or a visual
that forces the user to scroll before learning anything actionable.

## Composition and navigation

A compact horizontal masthead establishes the product, primary destinations,
and build or publication status without competing with the page. Content uses
a strong editorial grid, generous but purposeful spacing, and an ordinary
document flow that remains easy to scan at every viewport. Large type creates
clear entry points; rules, labels, and compact metadata establish rhythm and
provenance. Cards and bordered regions are used only when they explain a real
grouping or interaction, not as the default container for every sentence.

The page should feel dense with meaning rather than dense with chrome. A reader
must be able to distinguish the main takeaway, evidence summary, record
metadata, and next action at a glance. Long evidence material favors readable
single-column measure over dashboard grids that create gaps or fragment the
argument. Mobile is a first-class editorial composition, not a compressed
desktop layout.

## Color, type, and material

Graphite and near-black provide focus without turning the site into a glowing
chamber. Warm paper surfaces hold longer reading and evidence sections. Coral
is the human, physical accent for primary action and anatomical focus; teal
signals links, focus, and evidence-system structure. Both accents must remain
restrained and meet contrast requirements in their actual context. Status and
certainty are never communicated by color alone.

The primary type voice is a highly legible contemporary sans, with enough
weight range to move from commanding page titles to calm evidence prose. A
serif may appear rarely for a short editorial statement, but never at the cost
of clarity. Labels are compact, not microscopic; body copy is comfortably
readable; line lengths are controlled. Monospace is reserved for identifiers,
dates, or machine-like record details that benefit from it. Typography must do
most of the hierarchy work before decoration is added.

## Page archetypes

Each record family shares the same evidence language while retaining a distinct
visual job:

- **Muscle pages** pair a practical training summary with a clearly labeled
  anatomical orientation image, anatomy details, claim-level evidence, and
  related movements. The visual identifies the region; it does not pretend to
  prove a training claim.
- **Exercise pages** use a movement-specific schematic or approved technique
  media, not a recycled body specimen. They foreground what the exercise record
  covers, what the evidence supports, relevant limitations, and the related
  anatomy. A directional schematic is not a technique prescription and must say
  so.
- **Source pages** behave like compact research dossiers. Bibliographic
  identity, study design, population, status check, exact locators, supported
  claims, qualifications, and conflicts form one inspectable chain. Citation
  detail should look deliberate and readable, not like a footnote dump.

Related entities use canonical names and make the relationship explicit. The
shared components should make navigation predictable, but muscle, exercise,
and source pages must not collapse into an interchangeable card template.

## Anatomy-media hold

Production Blender, interactive 3D, and final body-media work remain on hold
until the owner gives further notice. SBLA-012 therefore uses the approved
static anatomy asset as an **evaluation plate**: a bounded, attributed visual
for layout and usability evaluation, not final production anatomy. The plate
must retain its source and license attribution, an accurate text alternative,
and visible evaluation status. It cannot be described as production-ready or
used as the sole route to content, navigation, or meaning.

The static plate should be framed cleanly and integrated with the page, without
attempting to simulate a finished 3D experience through glow, glass effects, or
fake controls. When the media hold is lifted, the production pipeline must
still satisfy the existing anatomy accuracy, licensing, deterministic build,
performance, accessibility, and review gates. This visual philosophy does not
weaken or bypass those gates.

## Evidence and publication integrity

Practical clarity never permits false certainty. Every substantive claim must
show the project-standard **certainty** term, applicability, important
limitations, and linked evidence as required by the record. Supporting,
qualifying, contradictory, and neutral source roles remain distinguishable.
Visual prominence may clarify a conclusion; it may not exaggerate one.

Review state and publication state remain separate. Unpublished prototype
content must retain the visible review-build warning, the exact
"not approved for publication" boundary, and `noindex,nofollow`. Ordinary
public builds must continue to fail closed: no visual treatment can expose a
claim that has not passed the established owner-approval, source-status,
checksum, certainty-language, and publication gates. Interface copy must remain
educational and must not imply diagnosis, individualized medical advice, or a
universally best exercise.

## Accessibility and interaction

The direction targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA and preserves semantic reading order,
keyboard access, visible focus, useful headings and landmarks, reduced-motion
support, sufficient text and non-text contrast, and accessible names for every
control. Text alternatives communicate the purpose of an image instead of
duplicating nearby prose. Decorative graphics are hidden from assistive
technology; meaningful graphics have an equivalent that does not depend on
vision, color, hover, WebGL, or pointer precision.

Disclosure controls clearly communicate their state, and expanded evidence
remains understandable in document order. Touch targets must be comfortably
sized. Motion, if introduced later, should explain change or spatial
relationship and must stop or simplify under reduced-motion preferences. The
static, semantic experience is a complete product path, not a fallback of last
resort.

## Quality bar

The finished system should feel authored, muscular, and calm: a premium
strength publication whose rigor is visible in both the practical answer and
the audit trail. Remove any element that only creates atmosphere. Add visual
complexity only when it improves orientation, comparison, evidence inspection,
or confidence in the record. The measure of polish is not spectacle; it is how
quickly a serious lifter can understand the answer, see its limits, verify the
work, and decide what to explore next.
